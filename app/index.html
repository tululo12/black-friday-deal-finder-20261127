How we check a deal — and how to spot a fake one

  1. Find it at the store. The price must be live on the retailer’s own page, or in a price check published 20–28 Nov 2026 that names the store.
  2. Compare it with the usual price. “Usual” is the retailer’s list price or the price the product sold at before the sale — never a number invented for the sale.
  3. Ask if it is really a deal. A 9% cut on a Nintendo bundle counts for more than a 50% cut off an inflated list price, because Nintendo almost never discounts. The badge on each card says which it is.
  4. Show the receipt. Every card names its source and the day it was checked, so you can verify it yourself before you buy.

The catch, honestly

Some retailers raise list prices before a sale, so a big “% off” can be mostly theatre — Amazon did this on some Echo, Fire TV and Kindle devices this year. That is why the “Lowest ever” badge only appears where a source says the price is the lowest tracked, and why small cuts are labelled small. Prices move fast in Black Friday week: always confirm the price at checkout.
